# How should Carshenas's sign-up, sign-in and account navigation work with a username and a password only?

- Date: 2026-09-29
- Asked by / for: CS-39. The owner decided on 2026-09-29 that buyers sign up and sign in with a username and a password only (no email, no phone number, no one-time code for now; phone and SMS code may come later); a superadmin lands on the superadmin dashboard after sign-in and a buyer returns to the page they came from; the header links visitors to sign-in and sign-up and gives a signed-in account a menu (profile, sign out, and the admin dashboard for a superadmin). He asked for "the most modern sign-in and register page patterns" and "the best UI/UX".
- Outcome: a recommendation for CS-39's interface (section "Recommendation"): two one-form pages on Server Actions, Latin case-insensitive usernames checked while typed, passwords of at least 8 characters (ADR-0020's draft; 15 is the alternative) with a hint that favours length, a live count and the browser's generator, a show button instead of a confirmation field, one generic sign-in failure with throttling messages, a validated `next`, a header account slot that streams in without moving anything, and the Farsi copy. It feeds ADR-0020 (proposed), agrees with the companion notes on most points, and names the few where it differs, with questions for the owner. No ADR is written here.
- Companion documents for CS-39, written the same day: [password accounts and sessions](2026-09-29-password-accounts-and-sessions.md) (hashing, the password policy's security side, sessions, cookies, throttling, the superadmin's bootstrap), [Iranian sign-in teardown](2026-09-29-iranian-sign-in-teardown.md) (Aparat, Filimo, Virgool, Torob and Namava), [sign-in and sign-up teardown](2026-09-29-sign-in-and-sign-up-teardown.md) (GitHub, Cal.com, Proton, Stripe, Supabase, Figma and Linear, captured), and the draft [ADR-0020](../decisions/0020-username-and-password-accounts.md) (proposed). This note is the interface: pages, fields, markup, errors, redirects, header, accessibility and Farsi copy. Where they overlap, the recommendation below says whether it agrees.

## Questions

1. Page and flow: separate sign-in and sign-up pages, or one identifier-first "continue" flow? How does each page switch to the other, what sits above the fold on a phone, and does the form work before JavaScript loads? What do Stripe, GitHub, Linear, Vercel, Clerk, Cal.com, Proton and Apple Account do?
2. Fields: which username rules (characters, length, case, trimming, Persian and Arabic digits), whether and how to check availability live, which password rules NIST SP 800-63B-4 and OWASP ASVS 5.0 impose on the interface, and what the evidence says about strength meters, a show-password toggle against a confirmation field, "remember me" and a Caps Lock warning.
3. Markup: what password managers and browsers need (`autocomplete`, `autocapitalize`, `autocorrect`, `spellcheck`, `inputmode`, `name` and `id`, `passwordrules`, `minlength` and `maxlength`, the form's method and action, navigation after success, `/.well-known/change-password`).
4. Validation and errors: when to validate, how to word errors, a summary or inline messages, where focus goes after a failed submit, `aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby` and live regions, a sign-in failure that does not say whether the username exists, a pending button that cannot submit twice, and a throttled or locked-out message.
5. After sign-in and sign-out: returning to the page the visitor came from through a validated same-origin `next` parameter, landing by role, where sign-out lives and whether it asks for confirmation.
6. Header and navigation for each auth state on phone and desktop, without layout shift when the server already knows the state.
7. Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), SC 3.3.7 Redundant Entry, visible labels, touch targets, and a 16 px input font.
8. Farsi and right-to-left: Farsi labels around left-to-right Latin inputs (`dir="ltr"` or `dir="auto"`), a warning when the keyboard is on Persian while typing a password, Persian digits in messages, and which words Iranian products use («ورود», «ثبت‌نام», «نام کاربری», «رمز عبور» or «گذرواژه», «خروج از حساب»).

### 1. Page and flow structure

- No product showed a Caps Lock warning in its markup or code.
- The identifier-first products all add hidden fields for password managers: Google a hidden password field on the identifier step, Apple the password input kept in the page, Clerk a hidden read-only `identifier` input on the password step commented "For password managers", Stripe a hidden `name=email autocomplete="username email"` input.
- Several leaders break the guidance collected here: Cal.com and Apple impose composition rules (NIST "SHALL NOT"), GitHub disables its submit button until the form is valid, Proton's toggle cannot be reached by keyboard, Clerk's sign-in password carries no `autocomplete` token, and GitHub's sign-up username uses `autocomplete="none"`, which is not a valid token. Copy the patterns, not the products.

- **Identifier-first is a routing device.** web.dev presents the two-page flow as an option, not a default: "Some sites (including Amazon and eBay) avoid the problem by asking for email (or phone) and password on two pages ... Ideally, this should be implemented with a single <form>", and "If you must force the user to navigate to a new page between entering their email and password, the form on the second page should have a hidden input element with the email value, to help enable password managers to store the correct value." GitLab's two-step sign-in (issue 572901) is driven by routing users to the right cell and to passkeys, and it keeps a generic "Invalid username and/or password" by making "every user ... try to at least enter a password" (read here through WebFetch's summary of the issue).
- **Switching between the two.** web.dev: "Many popular sites now display a single Sign in button. When the user taps or clicks, they also get a link to create an account, if necessary"; "don't hide login in a navigational menu"; and use "well-understood wording such as Sign In, Create Account or Register".
- **Above the fold on a phone.** web.dev: the mobile keyboard can hide the sign-in button, so "Where possible, avoid this by displaying only the email (or phone) and password inputs and Sign in button at the top of your sign-in page. Put other content underneath." For sign-up: "Keep it focused! Sign-up forms are not the place to distract people" and "make it clear what the value proposition is". The best sign-up is none: "Wherever possible you should avoid gating features behind login"; Carshenas's search, listing and model pages already render without an account (`listing-patterns.md`).
- **Before JavaScript.** "Server Components support progressive enhancement by default, meaning forms that call Server Actions will be submitted even if JavaScript hasn't loaded yet or is disabled" (`07-mutating-data.md`); in a Server Action `redirect` becomes a 303 without JavaScript (`redirecting.md`). With `useActionState` and a Server Function, React shows the server's answer before hydration, and "React automatically replays form submissions entered before hydration finishes" (react.dev, via Context7). Streaming has a limit: "When a boundary resolves, React streams a <div hidden id="S:0"> containing the completed HTML and a script that swaps it into the page" (`streaming.md`), so a form placed inside a Suspense boundary appears only once that inline script runs.
- **React 19 clears the form after every action.** "When the Form submission succeeds, React will automatically reset the form" (react.dev), and returning validation errors counts as success: issue #29034, "allow opting out of automatic form reset when Form Actions are used", opened 2024-05-09, is still open with 105 comments on 2026-09-29; #31649 ("Submitting a <form> with an action will clear the input values") was closed as not planned.
- **Cache Components change where the session is read.** This app sets `cacheComponents: true` (`apps/web/next.config.ts`). The Next.js guide: "A component that reads the session must sit behind a <Suspense> boundary. With Cache Components, reading cookies() outside a boundary is a build error", and "Keep the session read out of a layout's top level"; `use cache: private` lets a route that reads the session produce "a per-session App Shell ... prefetched and cached per session" (`authentication-with-cache-components.md`). Layouts "don't re-render on navigation", so the header must not be where access is checked (`authentication.md`).

### 2. Fields

#### Username

- **Google's guidance leans towards fewer and looser usernames.** web.dev: "Don't insist on a username unless (or until) you need one"; "If your site does require usernames, don't impose unreasonable rules on them, and don't stop users from updating their username. On your backend you should generate a unique ID for every user account, not an identifier based on personal data such as username"; "Also make sure to use autocomplete="username" for usernames"; and "it's best to allow usernames to include Unicode letters. Don't restrict to characters in the Latin alphabet" (Sign-up form best practices, 2020-12-09).
- **Case.** RFC 8265 §3.2: applying case mapping is "preferable ... because not doing so can lead to false accepts during authentication and authorization ... and can result in confusion among end users, given the prevalence of case mapping in many existing protocols and applications". Its UsernameCaseMapped profile maps full-width forms, lowercases with `toLowerCase()`, applies NFC, and applies the Bidi Rule of RFC 5893 to any string with right-to-left code points (§3.3.1). OWASP's cheat sheet said until at least 2023-12-18: "Make sure your usernames/user IDs are case-insensitive. User 'smith' and user 'Smith' should be the same user. Usernames should also be unique"; the current text keeps only that usernames "are easy-to-remember identifiers chosen by the user".
- **Reserved names.** ASVS 5.0 6.3.2 (Level 1): "Verify that default user accounts (e.g., "root", "admin", or "sa") are not present in the application or are disabled."
- **Persian letters are not one string per word** (lab, Unicode 13 tables): Arabic yeh «ي» (U+064A) and Persian yeh «ی» (U+06CC), Arabic kaf «ك» (U+0643) and keheh «ک» (U+06A9), Arabic-Indic and Persian digits, and Persian and Latin digits are all different under both NFC and NFKC. A Persian username would therefore need its own folding of «ي» and «ك» (the craft rule for search already does this, `craft.md` I-33), the Bidi Rule, and a confusables check; a Latin-only username needs none of them.
- **Typed digits and stray spaces.** The app already turns Persian and Arabic-Indic digits into Latin ones for anything typed (`apps/web/src/lib/digits.ts`, ADR-0014). GOV.UK: "accept information in different formats, as long as it's not ambiguous" and ignore unwanted characters entered "before or after an answer, as users might have copied and pasted them in by accident" (validation pattern).
- **Personal data.** A username made only of digits invites a phone number, which ADR-0013 point 8 treats as personal data; a rule that the first character is a letter keeps phone numbers out of the username column. (Inference.)

#### Checking availability while typing

- **The one field where live validation clearly pays.** Wroblewski's study: "Selecting a unique username, for example, often involves pogosticking: no one can know beforehand which usernames a website has available, so they guess, click "create account," find out the username they want is taken, guess again"; "For username and password questions, we used the "while" method with a short delay in each version we tested ... this method made the most sense for questions with strict boundaries, such as the set of usernames currently available or the required formatting for a secure password." The best variant gave "a 22% increase in success rates", "a 22% decrease in errors made", "a 31% increase in satisfaction rating" and "a 42% decrease in completion times" (A List Apart, 2009-09-01).
- NN/g, guideline 2: "when the user creates a username, a green checkmark and a message that the username is available provide clear feedback" (Krause, 2019, reviewed 2024-12-12). Friedman lists "a choice of an available username" among the cases where live feedback helps (Smashing, 2022-09-21).
- **It enumerates usernames, unavoidably.** OWASP's correct registration response ("A link to activate your account has been emailed to the address provided") needs an email loop, which Carshenas does not have; ASVS 5.0 6.3.8, which extends user-enumeration protection to registration, is Level 3 only. With usernames and no email, a sign-up form must say that a name is taken, so rate limiting the check is the control. A reader's comment under Wroblewski's article makes the same point: the username check "can be abused to perform username enumeration".
- **How GitHub's check behaves** (its published `@github/auto-check-element` 6.0.0, read in the sign-in and sign-up teardown note): a 300 ms debounce, the request in flight aborted when a newer one starts, a check when a changed field is left and on every keystroke only after a failed check. **Where it runs**: "Next.js dispatches Server Actions one at a time per client. If a user triggers three actions in quick succession, the second waits for the first to finish" (`server-actions.md`), so a check made as a Server Action would queue behind the form's own action and could not be cancelled; a Route Handler can be.
- **Announcing it.** A live region must exist before its content changes: "The most reliable way to ensure that live regions are registered is to include them in the initial markup"; `aria-live="polite"` waits "until the user is idle" (MDN, ARIA live regions).

#### Password rules that reach the interface

- NIST's appendix explains the length-first stance: composition rules produce "Password1!", blocklists should not be "excessively large", and "Other mitigations (e.g., blocklists, secure hashed storage, machine-generated random passwords, rate limiting) are more effective at preventing modern brute-force attacks" (Appendix A). Its usability section: "Allow at least 64 characters in length to support the use of passphrases" and "Provide clear, meaningful, and actionable feedback when chosen passwords are rejected" (§8).
- The OWASP Authentication cheat sheet adds: "If MFA is not enabled passwords shorter than 15 characters are considered to be weak", "Do not silently truncate passwords", "Allow usage of all characters including unicode and whitespace", and "Include a password strength meter", naming zxcvbn-ts.
- **Hashing sets the real maximum.** OWASP Password Storage: Argon2id with "19 MiB of memory, an iteration count of 2, and 1 degree of parallelism" first; bcrypt only for legacy, "with a password limit of 72 bytes". A Persian letter takes two bytes of UTF-8 and the zero-width non-joiner three, so a 64-character Persian password passes 128 bytes and bcrypt would truncate it, which NIST forbids.
- **NIST's 15 and ASVS's 8 disagree on purpose.** ASVS applies NIST's single-factor rule only as a strong recommendation at Level 1 and requires multi-factor authentication from Level 2 (V6.3 introduction), which is the point where NIST allows 8. GOV.UK asks for 8.
- **What a long minimum costs people.** Shay et al. (CHI 2014, 8,143 participants, eight policies): "most conditions are significantly more usable than comp8 on a number of metrics, with only basic20 and 3class16 being significantly less usable on any metric", so a 16-character minimum with no other rule (basic16) was not measurably harder than 12; "many participants fail to create a compliant password on their first try, suggesting that simple real-time feedback might benefit users who are required to create long passwords". Participants were online workers at computers, not people typing Latin on a phone whose first keyboard is Persian, where "entering a password character on mobile takes almost twice as much time than on the desktop" (NN/g, 2017, citing a University of Munich study).
- **What a long minimum does to the blocklist.** The companion note's lab: of 1,643,494 common passwords from three SecLists lists, 914,991 have at least 8 characters (3.4 MB gzipped) and only 16,135 have at least 15 (134 KB gzipped), so a 15-character minimum rules out almost every known common password by itself ([password accounts and sessions](2026-09-29-password-accounts-and-sessions.md), section 2).

#### Strength meters

- **They work when the account matters and the meter is strict.** Ur et al. (USENIX Security 2012, 2,931 participants, 14 meters): meters "led users to create longer passwords. However, significant increases in resistance to a password-cracking algorithm were only achieved using meters that scored passwords stringently", and participants who saw stringent meters "were also more likely to find the password meter annoying". Egelman et al. (CHI 2013): meters "yielded significantly stronger passwords" when people were forced to change real passwords, but when creating "a password for an unimportant account ... the meters made no observable difference: participants simply reused weak passwords that they used to protect similar low-risk accounts". Ur et al. (CHI 2017, 4,509 participants): a data-driven meter with detailed text feedback "led users to create more secure, and no less memorable, passwords than a meter with only a bar".
- **The standards stepped back.** ASVS 4.0.3 required one (2.1.8, all levels: "Verify that a password strength meter is provided"); ASVS 5.0 moved it to Appendix D, "should", "not ... mandatory". NIST requires guidance, not a meter. NN/g recommended meters (2015, 2017), citing Egelman 2013.
- **What one costs here** (lab, npm tarballs of 2026-09-29): zxcvbn-ts core 4.2.0 is 24,428 bytes gzipped and its common-language package 4.1.3 is 225,104 bytes gzipped, 204,142 of them the English-centric common-password list. The original Dropbox zxcvbn's last release is 4.4.2 (2017). A Carshenas account holds saved searches and marked listings, closer to Egelman's "unimportant" account than to a bank.

#### A show-password toggle, not a confirmation field

- **Showing.** Nielsen: "Usability suffers when users type in passwords and the only feedback they get is a row of bullets" (2009). Sherwin: on desktop hide by default with a "Show password" checkbox; on mobile show by default with a "Hide password" control (NN/g, 2015). Budiu, two years later: "we recommend masking the password by default and presenting users with a Show password checkbox" (NN/g, 2017). GOV.UK: "Hide passwords by default until the user chooses to show it using the 'show' button". WCAG's understanding of SC 3.3.8: "Providing a feature to optionally show a password can improve the chance of success for some people with cognitive disabilities".
- **Not confirming.** GOV.UK: "It's not necessary to add a second password field ... particularly as this component allows users to show and hide passwords." web.dev: asking twice "causes extra work for all users, and increases abandonment rates" and "makes no sense where browsers autofill ... or suggest strong passwords". NN/g (2017): "Do not repeat fields (e.g., two password fields, two email fields). Typing passwords is painful enough; typing them twice is twice as painful." Silver's registration form replaces the confirmation field with a show-password button (2018). WCAG SC 3.3.7 allows a second entry only as a security exception: "If the system requires the user to manually create a password that is not displayed, having users re-validate their new string is allowed".
- **GOV.UK's toggle in detail**: a button beside the input whose text is "Show" or "Hide", with the accessible names "Show password" and "Hide password", announcements "Your password is visible" and "Your password is hidden", `aria-controls` on the input, the button `hidden` in the HTML until its script runs, and "When the form is submitted, the password input should automatically change its type to password" so browsers do not store it as an ordinary autofill value. Known issue: browsers and password managers add their own show buttons, and a keyboard shortcut or a suggested password can show the password without the button changing. web.dev's example gives the button an accessible name that warns: "Show password as plain text. Warning: this will display your password on the screen." The APG button pattern allows two toggles, not a mix: with `aria-pressed`, "it is critical the label on a toggle does not change when its state changes"; if the label changes instead, "the aria-pressed attribute would not be needed".

#### "Remember me" and session length

- NIST at AAL1: "A definite reauthentication overall timeout SHALL be established, which SHOULD be no more than 30 days at AAL1. An inactivity timeout MAY be applied but is not required" (§2.1.3). ASVS 5.0 7.3.1 and 7.3.2 (L2) ask for an inactivity timeout and an absolute lifetime set by documented risk analysis; 7.2.4 (L1) for a new session token at every authentication.
- Baymard (2011): "Remember your user's sign in session for at least 14 days". web.dev: decide session length by "whether your users are on mobile or desktop, and whether they are sharing on desktop, or sharing devices".
- The products split: Stripe ("Remember me on this device"), Proton ("Keep me signed in", "Recommended on trusted devices.") and Apple offer the choice; GitHub, Clerk's components and Cal.com do not (survey in section 1).

#### Caps Lock and the keyboard's language

- `KeyboardEvent.getModifierState("CapsLock")` reports whether Caps Lock is locked (MDN). Phone keyboards send few key events a page can read, so the warning is a desktop aid (inference). None of the surveyed products shows one (section 1).
- NIST: "Subscribers choosing passwords that contain Unicode characters SHOULD be advised that some endpoints may represent some characters differently, which would affect their ability to authenticate successfully" (§3.1.1.2). The lab above shows the Persian case: the same Persian word typed on two keyboards can differ in «ی/ي» and «ک/ك», and the same digits typed on a Persian and an English layout are different strings. On the national layout the number row types Persian digits at the first level, the D key types «ی» and the semicolon key «ک», with Arabic «ي» and «ك» on other levels (ISIRI 9147), so a password with digits typed once on the Persian layout and once on the English one fails to match.
- (Iranian products' keyboard-language warnings are in section 8.)

### 3. Markup for browsers and password managers

- **One form per task, navigation after success.** Chromium: "Make sure that each authentication process (registration, login or change password) is grouped together in a single <form> element. Don't combine multiple processes (like registration and login), or skip the <form> element"; after a successful submission the page either "Performs a navigation to another page" or "Emulates a navigation with History.pushState or History.replaceState, and removes the password form completely"; "Don't try to fool the browser"; "Element id attributes should be unique" ("Create Amazing Password Forms", via sub-agent). web.dev: "Put sign-in in its own <form> element", "Give input name and id attributes stable values that don't change between page loads or website deployments", and use "different name and id values in sign-up and sign-in forms".
- **The tokens.** web.dev: `autocomplete="new-password"` with `id="new-password"` on sign-up and `autocomplete="current-password"` with `id="current-password"` on sign-in, and `autocomplete="username"` for the identifier. Apple: "the heuristics assume the user name and password inputs are on the same page. If you have a multipage login form, explicitly setting the username and current-password types lets the user tap and fill those inputs" (via sub-agent). WCAG SC 1.3.5 makes the tokens Level AA (section 7). For later passkeys, `webauthn` is added as the last token of the username's `autocomplete` for conditional autofill (MDN); Google and Apple already use `autocomplete="username webauthn"` on their identifier fields, and Google puts `dir="ltr"` on it (via sub-agent).
- **Multi-step flows need the username in the password form.** Chromium: "If you've implemented an 'email first' sign-in flow… include a form field containing the username in the form used to collect the password. You can, of course, hide this field via CSS" ("Password Form Styles that Chromium Understands", via sub-agent). The same applies to a change-password form.
- **Typing aids.** MDN: `autocapitalize` "none" or "off" stops virtual keyboards capitalising, has "no effect on the url, email, or password <input> types", and Chrome and Safari default to capitalising sentences elsewhere; `autocorrect` is always off for password, email and url inputs, but on by default for text inputs; `spellcheck="false"` asks the browser not to check spelling. GOV.UK sets `spellcheck="false"` and `autocapitalize` on its password input because a shown password is a text input, and some spell checkers sent field contents, "even user's passwords", to third parties ("spell-jacking"). `inputmode` needs no value: both fields take ordinary text.
- **Length attributes.** GOV.UK: "Do not use maxlength to restrict password length. Users will not get any feedback when they've reached the maxlength and their text input has been truncated. This happens when a user has pasted text from elsewhere or it's been autofilled by a password manager." Chrome's generator makes 15-character passwords of letters and digits without look-alike characters, shortened to the field's `maxlength` if one is set; it never reads `minlength` (Chromium `password_generator.cc`, via sub-agent). Safari's are "20 characters long. They contain one digit, one uppercase character, two hyphens, and 16 lowercase characters" (Apple Platform Security, via sub-agent). Both pass a rule of 15 characters or fewer with no composition requirement as they are; Chrome's has exactly 15.
- **`passwordrules` is Safari's.** Apple's syntax (`required:`, `allowed:`, `max-consecutive:`, `minlength:`) exists to describe composition rules to its generator, "The specified password length can't be less than 12", and "The hardest-to-guess password rule is allowed: unicode"; Chrome does not read the attribute and the WHATWG proposal (issue 3518) is still waiting for "implementer interest" (via sub-agent). A site without composition rules has nothing to tell it. GitHub sends `passwordrules="minlength: 15; allowed: unicode;"`.
- **HTML validation.** GOV.UK: turn it off with `novalidate` and "Do not add 'required' to your input fields"; web.dev: "Add the required attribute to both email and password fields". With `novalidate`, `required` no longer blocks submission and only tells assistive technology that the field is required. Native messages come in the browser's language, not the page's (inference).
- **`/.well-known/change-password`.** A W3C Working Draft of 2024-06-03: "Servers should redirect… with a redirect status of 302, 303, or 307", and "Servers must not locate the actual change password page at the change password url"; Safari has used it since 2019 and Chrome's password manager since version 86 (web.dev, 2020-09-01, via sub-agent). It matters only once a change-password page exists.

### 4. Validation and errors

- **When to validate: the sources disagree, in a pattern.** GOV.UK validates on submit only: "Do not validate when the user moves away from a field", and turns off browser validation: "add 'novalidate' to your form tags. Do not add 'required' to your input fields." Silver validates on submit only and never disables the submit button (2018). Wroblewski measured that validating after the user leaves a field beat validating while typing for ordinary questions, and that "before and while" produced "higher error rates and worse satisfaction ratings", but used "while, with a short delay" for the username and a new password (2009). NN/g: "avoid showing an error until the user has finished with the field and moved to the next field" (2019, guideline 7). Friedman: the earliest error is "when a user leaves a non-empty input field", empty fields are validated "only on submit", and a field already in error is re-checked on every keystroke so the error clears as soon as it is fixed ("reward early, punish late", after Konjević). The house rule in `mobile-forms.md` is this synthesis already.
- **After a failed submit.** GOV.UK: show the page again "with the form fields as the user filled them in", "add 'Error: ' to the beginning of the page <title> so screen readers read it out as soon as possible", show an error summary at the top "and move keyboard focus to it", and show an error message next to each field. Silver prefixes the title with the error count. WAI: the page title "can be useful to indicate successes and errors"; "If the submitted data contains errors, it is convenient to set the focus to the first <input> element that contains an error." NN/g: a summary must not be "the only indication of an error" (guideline 8). The house rule (`states-a11y.md`) asks for one of the two focus targets per form, `aria-describedby` from the field to its message, and `aria-invalid` only after validation ran.
- **Repeated failures.** NN/g guideline 10: when the same error happens "3 or more times in a single form-filling attempt", give extra help.
- **A failed sign-in says nothing about which field was wrong.** OWASP: respond "in a generic manner" whether "The user ID or password was incorrect", "The account does not exist" or "The account is locked or disabled", with the same HTTP status and about the same response time (hash even when the user does not exist); correct example: "Login failed; Invalid user ID or password." It admits the cost: "A legitimate user might feel confused with the generic messages". GOV.UK: "do not reveal whether they got the username or password wrong" and "Do not keep any entered password inputs from an incorrect login attempt". Baymard's 2011 advice was the opposite ("Tell them whether it's the password or the username that's wrong"); the security guidance since then is unanimous.
- **Throttling, not lockout.** NIST: at most 100 consecutive failures on one account, and ways to keep an attacker from locking the owner out, including "Requiring the claimant to wait after a failed attempt for a period of time that increases as the subscriber account approaches its maximum allowance ... (e.g., 30 seconds up to an hour)"; after a success the verifier "SHOULD disregard any previous failed attempts" (§3.2.2). OWASP: count failures per account, not per address; consider a lockout that doubles; beware lockout used as denial of service. GOV.UK: "Give users between 5 and 10 attempts to enter their password correctly before you lock their account or do any further security checks." WCAG SC 3.3.8 applies to a CAPTCHA shown only "after repeated incorrect password entry" as much as to one shown every time (section 7).
- **One press, one submission.** web.dev: "Consider disabling the Sign in button once the user has tapped or clicked it. Many users click buttons multiple times", but "don't disable form submission awaiting user input". The house pattern (`react-patterns`, `data-and-actions.md` §4) keeps the button focusable with `aria-disabled`, ignores repeat presses in the handler, and fades a spinner in only after the pending delay.

### 5. After sign-in and sign-out

- **The return address is an open-redirect risk.** Microsoft describes the attack on exactly this flow: a link to the sign-in page with `returnUrl=http://contoso1.com/...` sends a freshly signed-in user to a look-alike site that asks for the password again. Its `IsLocalUrl` accepts a URL only when it "doesn't have the host or authority section" and "has an absolute path". OWASP: "do not allow the URL as user input for the destination"; if unavoidable, validate it against an allow-list. ASVS 5.0 3.7.2 (L2): redirect automatically to another host only if it is on an allow-list.
- **Next.js mechanics.** In a Server Action, `redirect` "performs a client-side navigation when JavaScript is available. Without JavaScript, the form submission uses a 303 (See Other)" (`redirecting.md`). Server Actions accept only POST and compare the Origin header with the Host header (`data-security.md`). `<Link>` prefetches its target "when a <Link /> component enters the user's viewport", in production (`link.md`): a sign-out reachable by GET would sign people out when the menu renders.
- **Sessions.** ASVS 5.0 7.4.1 (L1): after logout or expiry "the application disallows any further use of the session", which for server-side sessions means deleting them on the server; 7.4.4 (L2): "all pages that require authentication have easy and visible access to logout functionality".
- **No confirmation for sign-out.** The house rule: "Confirmation only for destructive or costly actions" (`mobile-forms.md`); NN/g: use confirmation dialogs "sparingly" (2019, guideline 6). Signing out loses nothing that signing in does not restore.
- **The admin link.** CS-40 requires admin pages to be "never linked from public pages" and to answer 404 to anyone without the superadmin role. An item in the account menu that renders only for a superadmin is not a public link, and the owner asked for it.

### 6. Header and navigation

- **A visible, worded entry.** web.dev: "Make it obvious how to create an account on your site, for example with a Login or Sign in button at the top right of the page. Avoid using an ambiguous icon or vague wording", and "Many popular sites now display a single Sign in button" that leads to both. In a right-to-left layout the mirror of "top right" is the top left, the header's inline end, since the logo takes the start (`rtl-bidi.md`: layouts mirror).
- **Signed in: a menu button or a disclosure.** The APG menu button opens a `role="menu"` whose items may be links ("Navigation Menu Button" example) and brings the menu keyboard model (arrows, typeahead, Escape). The APG's disclosure navigation example deliberately avoids the menu role for site navigation because "it does not provide the complex functionality that assistive technologies expect in a widget that has the menu role". An account menu that mixes two or three links with a sign-out action fits the menu button; the house craft rules already require the APG keyboard model for menus and Base UI's `DirectionProvider` for right-to-left arrow keys (craft I-18; ADR-0005; design language section 6).
- **Logout must be easy to reach** from every page that needs an account (ASVS 5.0 7.4.4).
- **The state is known on the server, but only inside a boundary.** Under Cache Components the header's static shell cannot contain the session; the account slot streams in behind `<Suspense>` (section 1). The house rules budget zero layout shift for designed swaps and reserve a fixed box around late blocks (craft L-1, L-4), which here means a slot of fixed size whose fallback is the same empty box.
- (Iranian products' header wording is in section 8.)

### 7. Accessibility

- **SC 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), AA.** "Remembering a site-specific password is a cognitive function test", allowed when "A mechanism is available to assist the user", for example "support for password entry by password managers" and "copy and paste". A login form passes when it meets SC 1.3.5 and 4.1.2 so that "user agents and password managers should be able to reliably recognize the fields and automatically fill them in"; blocking autofill or paste fails it. The criterion covers signing in, not "creation of a username or initiation of an account", though the same techniques help there. A CAPTCHA that is not object recognition needs an alternative method, and "This criterion applies when these tests are used regardless of whether they are used every time or only triggered by specific scenarios", such as "after repeated incorrect password entry".
- **SC 3.3.7 Redundant Entry, A.** The confirmation-field exception above; and "A user submits a checkout form with an incorrect credit card number in it. The page updates, showing an error message. Submitted information ... is not cleared from the form." A password may be cleared under the security exception; the username may not.
- **SC 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose, AA.** WCAG 2.2 section 7 lists `username`, `new-password` ("when creating an account or changing a password") and `current-password` ("when logging in"), so the `autocomplete` tokens are a Level AA requirement, not a nicety.
- **Visible labels.** web.dev: "Don't use placeholders as input labels" and "It's probably best to put your labels above your inputs"; Silver: hint text above the field instead of placeholders. The house rules say the same (`mobile-forms.md`).
- **Size.** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 asks for 24 × 24 CSS px; the house minimum is 44 px, with 48 px for the primary action (`mobile-forms.md`; `min-h-11` and `min-h-12` in the design language). "If the font-size of an <input> is 16px or larger, Safari on iOS will focus into the input normally. But as soon as the font-size is 15px or less, the viewport will zoom into that input" (Coyier, 2021); the `text-control` role is 16 px.

### 8. Farsi and right-to-left

- **Left-to-right inputs inside a right-to-left page.** The house rule: fields whose content stays left to right carry `dir="ltr"` and stay left-aligned, while search boxes use `dir="auto"` (`rtl-bidi.md`); Wikimedia Codex: phone numbers "should never be mirrored and must remain left-aligned in both LTR and RTL layouts"; Google's identifier field carries `dir="ltr"` (section 3). A username limited to Latin characters and a password are always left to right, so `dir="ltr"` puts the caret at the left and keeps a pasted value in order, while the label, hint and messages stay outside the box, right-aligned, in Persian. A placeholder would follow the input's direction and sit at the left; the house rules treat placeholders as examples only, so none is needed.

## Recommendation

Two short pages, `/sign-in` and `/sign-up`, each one form with the username and the password on the same screen, posted to a Server Action so it works before JavaScript loads, each linking to the other and carrying the visitor's `next` through both. Usernames are Latin, case-insensitive and checked for availability while they are typed. Passwords are at least 8 characters, as ADR-0020 (proposed) decides, with a hint that favours length, a live count of what is missing and the browser's generator; they are shown on request and never typed twice. A failed sign-in names neither field, and repeated failures say how long to wait. After signing in, a buyer goes back to `next` and a superadmin to the dashboard. The header shows «ورود / ثبت‌نام» («ورود» on narrow phones) to visitors and an account button with a menu to signed-in people, streamed into a slot of fixed size so nothing moves.

### 1. Pages and flow

- **Two pages, not an identifier-first flow** (both teardown notes agree). Identifier-first earns its extra step when the identifier decides what comes next (single sign-on, a passkey, a data-centre cell, a phone code); with a username and a password only, nothing is routed, and the split would also need a hidden username field on the second step for password managers (section 1). Revisit when phone sign-in arrives: a phone-first "continue" flow that signs in or signs up is the Iranian norm there (section 8).
- **Each page, top to bottom on a phone:** the site header with the logo only (no account slot on these two pages); the heading; on `/sign-up` only, one line on what an account is for; the two fields; the full-width primary button (48 px, never disabled); the line that switches to the other page; nothing else. With the keyboard open, the button stays in view under the password field (web.dev). Below the button is where GitHub, Stripe, Clerk, Cal.com and Proton put the switch; the teardown note proposes the top, as Namava does. On pages this short both are above the fold: pick one and use it on both pages. On desktop the same column, `max-w-sm` (24rem) and centred, with the teardown note's token mapping.
- **One `<form>` per page**, `method="post"` bound to a Server Action through `useActionState`, `noValidate` (our Farsi messages, not the browser's), a hidden `next` input, `id="sign-in"` and `id="sign-up"`. Without JavaScript the form posts and the server answers with the page and its errors, or a 303 to the landing page (Next.js). Keep the form in the page's static shell; only the `next` value and the "already signed in" redirect read the request, inside their own small boundary, because streamed boundaries are revealed by an inline script.
- **Keep what the person typed.** React 19 clears uncontrolled fields after every form action, even one that returned errors (issue #29034, still open), so the action's state returns the normalised username and the field takes it as its default value; the password is never returned and is cleared, which GOV.UK asks for after a failed sign-in and WCAG SC 3.3.7 allows as a security exception.
- **Already signed in:** either page redirects to the landing page for that account (section 5).
- **Switching:** «حساب ندارید؟» with the link «ثبت‌نام کنید» on `/sign-in`, and «حساب دارید؟» with «وارد شوید» on `/sign-up`; both keep `next`. When the live check says a username is taken, its message also links to `/sign-in`, since the person may already have that account.

### 2. Sign-up fields

**Username**

```html
<label for="username">نام کاربری</label>
<p id="username-hint">…</p>
<input id="username" name="username" type="text" dir="ltr"
       autocomplete="username" autocapitalize="none" autocorrect="off" spellcheck="false"
       required aria-describedby="username-hint username-message">
<p id="username-message" aria-live="polite"></p>
```

- **Rules**, the same as the companion note's and ADR-0020's: trim spaces at both ends; turn Persian and Arabic-Indic digits into Latin (`toLatinDigits`); lowercase; then only `a` to `z`, `0` to `9` and `_`, 3 to 30 characters, starting with a letter (which also keeps phone numbers out). The database enforces the format and uniqueness, and the insert's unique violation becomes «این نام کاربری گرفته شده است» (ADR-0013: never check, then insert). Reserved names (`admin`, `administrator`, `superadmin`, `root`, `system`, `support`, `carshenas`, `karshenas` and similar) get the same "taken" message (ASVS 6.3.2).
- No `maxlength` attribute (a pasted or autofilled value would be cut without a word, GOV.UK); the length is a message.
- A Persian letter typed into the field shows the keyboard hint at once, before any other rule (section 8).
- **Availability while typing:** once the value passes the local rules, wait 300 to 400 ms after the last keystroke (GitHub waits 300, Cal.com about 600), abort any request still in flight, ask a rate-limited Route Handler (not a Server Action, which would queue behind the form's own), and write the answer into the always-present polite region, one line tall so a result never moves the button, the "available" line with a check icon (wording in section 7). Show no "checking" text to screen readers; a small spinner may fade in after the pending delay (craft L-13). The check is advice; the insert decides. Without JavaScript, a taken name is reported on submit.

**Password**

```html
<label for="new-password">رمز عبور</label>
<p id="new-password-hint">…</p>
<div class="relative">
  <input id="new-password" name="new-password" type="password" dir="ltr"
         autocomplete="new-password" autocapitalize="none" autocorrect="off" spellcheck="false"
         required aria-describedby="new-password-hint new-password-count new-password-message">
  <button type="button" aria-controls="new-password" aria-label="نمایش رمز عبور" hidden>…</button>
</div>
<p id="new-password-count" aria-live="polite"></p>
<p id="new-password-message"></p>
```

- **No `minlength`, `maxlength` or `passwordrules`:** with `noValidate` the first does nothing, the second cuts a pasted or autofilled password without a word (GOV.UK) and, if short, Chrome's generated one too, and the third only describes composition rules to Safari, which we do not have.
- **Policy, from ADR-0020 (proposed) and the companion note:** 8 to 128 characters counted as code points after normalisation; any characters, spaces and Persian included; no composition rules; NFC, non-ASCII spaces to U+0020, «ي» and «ك» to «ی» and «ک», and Persian and Arabic-Indic digits to Latin before the blocklist and the hash, at sign-up and sign-in alike, with no trimming and no case folding; refused when it is one of the 100,000 most common passwords (the NCSC list, also after lowercasing and after mapping keys typed with the Persian layout back to Latin) or built from the username or the site's name. A rejection says why and how to do better (NIST SHALL).
- **What the interface adds so that length comes easily:**
  - the hint sells length, not complexity: a few words with spaces, or the browser's suggestion (section 7);
  - a live count of what is missing, as Shay et al. suggest for long minimums: under the field, «۳ کاراکتر دیگر» while the password is short, updated as the person types and announced politely only after a pause, gone once the minimum is met; it is feedback, not an error, and it never turns red;
  - `autocomplete="new-password"`, so Chrome (15 characters) and Safari (20) offer a generated password that passes at once, and would still pass if the minimum ever rose to 15.
- **Show and hide**, as the Iranian teardown note proposes and the international one agrees: an icon button inside the field at its right edge (the input's own end, where Aparat, Filimo and Namava put field icons), with a 44 px hit area, visible even while the field is empty, and the input reserving its room with padding on the inline end. Its accessible name changes from «نمایش رمز عبور» to «پنهان کردن رمز عبور», with no `aria-pressed` (APG: a toggle's label must not change), and a polite announcement «رمز عبور نمایش داده می‌شود» or «رمز عبور پنهان شد» (GOV.UK). It is `hidden` in the server HTML until hydration, in a slot whose space is already reserved so nothing moves (craft L-1), and the input goes back to `type="password"` on submit.
- **No confirmation field and no strength meter.** The hint, the live count, the blocklist message and the browser's generator are the guidance NIST requires; for accounts like these a meter changed nothing in Egelman's field study and would cost 225 KB. Reconsider a lazily loaded zxcvbn-ts if accounts start to hold something worth attacking.
- **Warnings, never errors:** a Persian letter typed into the password field (Persian digits are folded, so they need none; section 8), and Caps Lock on a physical keyboard, read with `getModifierState("CapsLock")`.

### 3. Sign-in fields

- The same username field (`id="username"`, `autocomplete="username"`, same normalisation). The password is `id="current-password"`, `name="current-password"`, `autocomplete="current-password"`, with no count and no `minlength` (the browser must never judge an existing password), the same toggle and the same warnings.
- No "remember me": a buyer's session lasts 30 days from sign-in (NIST's AAL1 limit) with no inactivity timeout, as ADR-0020's draft decides, and «خروج از حساب» is always one menu away.

### 4. Validation, errors and throttling

- **The server decides; the client repeats only the format rules**, following the house timing: nothing while a field is first typed except the username's availability, the password's count and the keyboard warnings; a non-empty field is checked when it loses focus; a field already in error is re-checked on every keystroke so the error clears as soon as it is fixed; empty fields and the blocklist only on submit.
- **After a failed submit:** the title becomes «خطا: …» (GOV.UK), an error summary under the heading lists each problem as a link to its field and receives focus (`tabindex="-1"`), and each field shows its own message under it, tied with `aria-describedby`, with `aria-invalid="true"` only now. One focus target for both pages: the summary.
- **A failed sign-in** shows only the summary: «نام کاربری یا رمز عبور درست نیست.», the wording of the password note and the Iranian teardown note. The server verifies against a dummy hash for unknown usernames and answers with the same status, so time and code say nothing either (OWASP). After three failures in a row the summary adds help (NN/g guideline 10) about the keyboard, Caps Lock and signing up (section 7).
- **Throttling** is the design of the companion note and ADR-0020's draft: counters per normalised username whether or not the account exists, per device cookie so the owner keeps signing in while someone else guesses, and per address; no wait for the first five failures, then 30 seconds doubling up to an hour. The interface's part is the message: the real remaining time in Persian digits, in words that do not imply the account exists: «چند بار پشت سر هم ورود ناموفق بود. ۸ دقیقه‌ی دیگر دوباره امتحان کنید.» No CAPTCHA: a text or puzzle CAPTCHA needs an alternative under WCAG SC 3.3.8 even when it appears only after failures, and any provider needs an ADR that proves it works from Iran.
- **Pending:** the house button (`aria-disabled`, repeat presses ignored, the spinner after the pending delay, label and width unchanged); fields stay editable.
- **Logs** never carry the typed username or the password (companion note, recommendation point 9).

### 5. After sign-in, sign-up and sign-out

- **`next`:** accepted only as a path on our own origin: a string of at most 2,048 characters that starts with `/` but not `//` or `/\`, that `new URL(next, origin)` resolves to our origin, and that is not `/sign-in`, `/sign-up` or the sign-out action; anything else is dropped silently (Microsoft's `IsLocalUrl`, OWASP; the companion note states the same rule). The header link, every "sign in to …" prompt, both switch links and the forms' hidden input carry it.
- **Landing:** a superadmin goes to the admin dashboard, or to `next` when it points inside the admin section; a buyer goes to `next`, or home. A buyer's `next` into the admin section is dropped. Signing up signs the person in and lands them by the same rule, with no welcome page: the header's new state is the confirmation. The navigation after success is also what lets Chrome and Safari offer to save the password (web.dev, Chromium).
- **Sign-out** is a POST Server Action, reachable from the account menu and as a button on the account page (so it works without the menu's script, ASVS 7.4.4); it deletes the session on the server (ASVS 7.4.1) and lands on the same page when that page is public, otherwise home. Never a link: `<Link>` prefetches in production. No confirmation dialog, although Digikala, Divar and Torob ask one: they sign people in mainly with a code sent by SMS, so for them signing out is costly, while here the menu already takes two deliberate taps and a password manager signs the person back in. Aparat's after-the-fact line, «از حساب کاربری خارج شدید.», is the better local model, shown once on the landing page if it is cheap to build.

### 6. Header and account menu

- **Visitors:** a text link at the header's inline end (the left), 44 px tall, to `/sign-in?next=<this page>`, the path and query taken from `usePathname()` and `useSearchParams()` inside the slot's boundary. «ورود» on narrow phones and «ورود / ثبت‌نام» where it fits (the Iranian teardown note; Digikala's compact header does the same), chosen by CSS so the server sends one markup. Words, not an icon (web.dev: "Avoid using an ambiguous icon").
- **Signed in:** a 44 px button with a person icon and the accessible name «منوی حساب کاربری», the same size on phone and desktop; the username appears at the top of the menu, in `<span dir="ltr">`, not in the header, so the button never changes width. It opens a menu (shadcn's dropdown on Base UI, which makes it the app's first interactive primitive: the root layout gains Base UI's `DirectionProvider` with `direction="rtl"`, ADR-0005 and the design language section 6) with the APG menu-button keyboard model (craft I-18):
  1. the username, as a heading of the menu, not an item;
  2. «حساب کاربری», a link to `/account`;
  3. «پنل مدیریت», a link to the dashboard, rendered only for a superadmin;
  4. a separator, then «خروج از حساب», which submits the sign-out form.
- **No flash and no shift:** with `cacheComponents: true` the session can only be read behind a `<Suspense>` boundary, so the header's static shell prerenders and only the account slot streams in. The slot has a fixed size at each breakpoint (the visitor link's), its fallback is an empty box of that size, and the signed-in button aligns to its inline end, so nothing in the header moves (craft L-1, L-4). The session is never fetched from the client. `getCurrentUser` uses `use cache: private` so per-session shells prefetch (Next.js guide).
- On `/sign-in` and `/sign-up` the slot is not rendered.

### 8. Leave out now

- Email, phone number and one-time codes (the owner's decision), and with them email verification and SMS recovery.
- Federated sign-in (Google, GitHub), magic links and passkeys. The markup above is ready for passkeys: adding `webauthn` to the username's `autocomplete` enables conditional autofill later (MDN), as Google and Apple already do.
- A confirmation field, a strength meter, "remember me", security questions, hints, periodic password changes, and CAPTCHA.
- Identifier-first flows and a combined "sign in or sign up" page: the right shape once phone codes arrive, as Divar, Torob and Digikala show (section 8).
- Self-service recovery: with no email or phone, NIST's only self-service method would be a saved recovery code shown once at sign-up (§4.2.1.1), and support cannot tell the owner of a username from anyone else. ADR-0020's draft: none until phone sign-in arrives (section 10).
- Username changes and account deletion: later, from the account page.

### 9. Trade-offs, and what would change them

- **8 characters for buyers, as ADR-0020 (proposed) decides, not NIST's 15, which the companion note recommends.** For 15: NIST's SHALL for a password used alone, Shay et al.'s finding that a 16-character minimum without other rules was not measurably harder than 12, generators that already make 15 and 20, and a blocklist that shrinks from about 915,000 entries to 16,135. For 8: ASVS Level 1 and GOV.UK accept it; with no recovery, a forgotten password loses the account, ADR-0020's reason; whoever types a password rather than letting a manager fill it does so on a phone whose first keyboard is usually Persian (inference), where each character takes about twice as long as on a desktop; and nobody has measured 15 there. The 100,000-entry blocklist and throttling carry the security of a low-value account. The interface differs by three strings, so the choice can change late.
- **Latin-only usernames against web.dev's advice to allow Unicode letters** (all three notes agree). Telegram, whose usernames are probably the ones Iranian users have made before (inference), allows only a to z, 0 to 9 and the underscore and says so in its Persian FAQ (section 8); the field stays left to right, and it avoids «ي/ی» and «ك/ک» in identities, the Bidi Rule and confusables. A display name in Persian, if public profiles ever exist, is a separate field.
- **Folding before hashing departs from the letter of ASVS 6.2.8** ("exactly as received"): the companion note's decision, permanent for stored hashes. It removes failures that only Persian and Arabic keyboards cause; the interface's keyboard warning covers the one it cannot, a Latin password typed with the Persian layout on.
- **No strength meter.** Egelman's finding for low-value accounts and 225 KB of dictionary argue against it now; the evidence for data-driven meters (Ur 2017) argues for revisiting it if passwords start to matter.
- **No sign-out confirmation**, unlike Digikala, Divar and Torob; revisit if people report signing out by accident.
- **Usernames can be enumerated** through the availability check and sign-up. That is inherent to usernames without email; rate limits keep it slow. It would matter more once profiles are public or accounts are valuable.

### 10. For the owner to decide, and notes on ADR-0020's draft

- **Buyers' minimum length:** 8 (ADR-0020's draft, and this note's choice for the interface) or 15 (NIST; the companion note's recommendation, its open question 1).
- **Recovery:** none until phone numbers arrive (ADR-0020's draft), or a recovery code shown once after sign-up. With none, the sign-up page says so, and the sign-in page carries the `<details>` explanation above: it is not a recovery link that cannot work, which the draft rules out, but the answer a stuck person is looking for; drop it if the owner prefers silence.
- **Where a superadmin lands when `next` is a public page:** ADR-0020's draft lets `next` win for everyone; the owner's words ("an account with the superadmin role lands on the superadmin dashboard") read as the dashboard unless `next` points inside the admin section. Either is one line of code; the owner picks.
- **The "taken" message:** the draft's «این نام کاربری را کس دیگری برداشته است» assumes someone else took the name, but the person may already hold that account; «این نام کاربری گرفته شده است. نام دیگری انتخاب کنید یا وارد شوید.» is true either way and offers the way in.
- **«کاراکتر» or «نویسه»** in length rules: «کاراکتر» is what Divar, Aparat and Quera say and «نویسه» has no standalone entry in the Academy's dictionary, so «کاراکتر» is recommended; the teardown note leaves it open.
- **Change password** is a follow-up in ADR-0020's draft (and one of the companion note's five Server Actions; ASVS 5.0 6.2.2 and 6.2.3, Level 1). Its interface: the current password (`autocomplete="current-password"`), the new one (`autocomplete="new-password"`, with the hint and count above), a hidden username field for password managers (Chromium), and `/.well-known/change-password` redirecting to it with a 302 (W3C draft).
